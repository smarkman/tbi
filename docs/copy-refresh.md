# How to use this document

- Every fact comes from the current website. Nothing has been invented. Anything that may be out of date is listed under **Please confirm** at the end, and is marked **[CONFIRM]** where it appears.
- Text in **[BRACKETS]** needs a detail only TBI can supply.

# Voice and style

**Who we sound like:** a warm, confident congregation that is proud of its 90 years and glad you're here. Polished but never stiff. Think of a thoughtful member welcoming a guest at kiddush, not a brochure and not a bulletin board.

**Do**

- Speak to the reader as "you", and about the community as "we".
- Lead with what someone gets or can do, then the details.
- Be specific: times, ages, grades, names. Specifics build trust.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences. Use headings and short lists.
- Translate a Hebrew term the first time it appears on a page, for example *kehillah* (community). After that, use it freely.
- Write buttons as clear actions: **Plan a visit**, **Schedule a tour**, **Register**, **Donate**.

**Don't**

- Use filler sign-offs like "Want to learn more... Get involved... Share ideas?" End with one clear action and one named contact.
- Use "click here", "read more" or ALL CAPS.
- Use phrases that date quickly ("this year", "recently", "over two years ago"). Use the year, or leave the timing out.
- Put personal email addresses or phone extensions in body copy. Use role addresses (info@, preschool@, director@) or a contact form.
- Paste in formatting from Word or Google Docs (see the editor rules in the UX review).

**House style**

- Temple Beth Israel on first mention, then TBI.
- B'nai Mitzvah (plural), Bar Mitzvah, Bat Mitzvah. Use B'nai Mitzvah for programs.
- Shabbat, Kiddush, Oneg, Havdalah, Tikkun Olam: capitalized, not italicized.
- High Holy Days (not High Holidays) in headings. Hanukkah (not Chanukah). Use one spelling everywhere.
- Times: 6:15 pm, 9:30 am. Days spelled out: Friday, not Fri.
- Phone: (516) 767-1708.

# About

## Welcome to Temple Beth Israel

Temple Beth Israel is an egalitarian, contemporary Conservative synagogue, and the first synagogue in Port Washington. For 90 years we have been a spiritual home for families, couples and individuals in Port Washington and beyond.

We are a *kehillah*, a community, of more than 300 households. Some of us come every Shabbat. Some come for the High Holy Days, the preschool or a book talk. All of us are part of the TBI family.

**Our values**

- **Klal Yisrael:** unity within the diversity of the Jewish people
- **Tefillah and Chinuch:** prayer, ritual, education and study
- **Kavod, Kehillah and Menschlichkeit:** dignity, community and decency in how we treat one another

**Everyone belongs here**

Traditional and non-traditional households, single parents, blended families, interfaith couples, LGBTQ+ members and families with special needs all find a warm welcome at TBI. However you build your Jewish life, we're here to celebrate it with you.

# Life events

Our clergy are with you at life's most important moments, from the joyful to the hardest. To begin, contact the office at (516) 767-1708 or info@tbiport.org.

- **Welcoming a baby.** We celebrate a son's *brit milah* (covenant of circumcision) on the eighth day, and a daughter's entry into the covenant at a baby naming (*simchat bat*) where she receives her Hebrew name. Our clergy will help you plan either.
- **B'nai Mitzvah.** Becoming a Bar or Bat Mitzvah marks a child's move from learning about responsibility to taking it on. Our clergy and Religious School educators prepare each student personally, and families are guided every step of the way.
- **Weddings.** From the aufruf, the Shabbat blessing before the wedding, to the ketubah and the chuppah, our clergy will help you design a ceremony that reflects who you are.
- **Conversion.** Choosing Judaism is a profound journey, and it begins with learning. Our clergy have guided many people through it and welcome your questions.
- **Illness and healing.** Call our clergy when someone you love is ill or facing a procedure, and we will include their name in the Mi Shebeirach, our prayer for healing.
- **Loss and mourning.** Our rabbis officiate at funerals and unveilings and will guide your family through Jewish mourning customs. Our community surrounds mourners with care. Volunteers lead shiva minyanim and provide condolence meals.

## Religious School

**Strong Jewish roots, joyfully grown**

TBI Religious School gives children a love of Jewish life and the skills to live it. Students learn to read Hebrew, celebrate the holidays and prepare for B'nai Mitzvah through art, music, food, games and hands-on projects. We hope every child starts with us in Shorashim (kindergarten) and stays through Confirmation in 10th grade.

**Grade by grade**

- **Shorashim (K):** the Hebrew alphabet, Shabbat and the creation story, through song, dance and art.
- **Kitah Alef (1st):** holidays through Torah stories, the aleph-bet through movement, and a first "visit" to Israel.
- **Kitah Bet (2nd):** twice-weekly learning, with a Tuesday Hebrew session on Zoom. Ends with each child receiving their own siddur (prayer book) at a family ceremony.
- **Kitah Gimel (3rd):** leading Havdalah, journaling about big Jewish ideas, and growing Hebrew and prayer skills.
- **Kitah Dalet (4th):** Jewish history comes alive, ending in our Living Wax Museum.
- **Kitah Hei (5th):** family roots, and Jewish life around the world through music, art and food.
- **Kitah Vav (6th):** midrash, the Mitzvot Fair, and a Haftarah trope boot camp to begin B'nai Mitzvah preparation.
- **Kitah Zayin (7th):** Israel today through media and guest speakers, with Hebrew focused on B'nai Mitzvah.

## The Morashah Society

*Morashah* means inheritance: what one generation hands to the next. Members of the Morashah Society have included TBI in their will, trust or estate plans, helping make sure Port Washington's first synagogue remains a vibrant home for Conservative Judaism for generations to come.

*"Our success is due to the generosity of our members. I hope to add to our future success by leaving a gift to TBI in my will."* (Rita Shumsky, member since 1990 and past president)

Anyone can leave a legacy, whatever their age or means. Morashah Society members are recognized each year during the High Holy Days and invited to special TBI programs.
